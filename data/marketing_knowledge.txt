DIGITAL MARKETING KNOWLEDGE BASE

CTR - Click Through Rate

CTR measures the percentage of users who clicked an advertisement after seeing it.

CTR = Clicks / Impressions × 100

A low CTR may indicate weak ad creatives, irrelevant messaging, poor headlines, weak calls-to-action, or poor audience targeting.

A high CTR usually indicates that the advertisement is relevant and attractive to the target audience.

If CTR is low, marketers should test different headlines, images, videos, ad copy, calls-to-action, and audience segments.


CONVERSION RATE

Conversion Rate measures the percentage of clicks that result in conversions.

Conversion Rate = Conversions / Clicks × 100

A low conversion rate can happen when the landing page is poorly designed, the offer is weak, the checkout process is complicated, or the audience is not highly relevant.

A high conversion rate generally indicates that the traffic, offer, and landing page are working effectively.

If conversion rate is low, marketers should improve landing page experience, simplify the conversion process, strengthen the offer, and test different calls-to-action.


ROAS - Return on Ad Spend

ROAS measures how much revenue is generated for every unit of advertising spend.

ROAS = Revenue / Advertising Spend

A high ROAS indicates strong advertising efficiency.

A low ROAS indicates that advertising spend is not generating sufficient revenue.

Low ROAS can result from high advertising costs, poor targeting, low conversion rates, weak offers, or low-quality traffic.

If ROAS is low, marketers should review campaign targeting, creatives, landing pages, conversion rates, and budget allocation.


CPA - Cost Per Acquisition

CPA measures the average advertising cost required to generate one conversion.

CPA = Advertising Spend / Conversions

A high CPA indicates that acquiring customers is expensive.

A low CPA generally indicates efficient customer acquisition.

CPA can increase because of expensive advertising, poor targeting, low CTR, low conversion rate, or inefficient landing pages.

If CPA is high, marketers should optimize targeting, improve ad creatives, improve conversion rates, and reduce inefficient spending.


AUDIENCE TARGETING

Audience targeting determines which users see a marketing campaign.

Relevant targeting can improve CTR, conversion rate, and ROAS.

Poor targeting can result in low CTR, low conversion rate, high CPA, and low ROAS.

If campaign performance is weak across multiple KPIs, audience relevance should be reviewed.


AD CREATIVE

Ad creative includes images, videos, headlines, descriptions, and calls-to-action.

Strong creative can increase user attention and CTR.

Weak or repetitive creative can reduce engagement and CTR.

If CTR is below the target level, marketers should test new creatives, headlines, messages, formats, and calls-to-action.


LANDING PAGE EXPERIENCE

Landing page experience has a major impact on conversion rate.

A slow, confusing, or poorly designed landing page can cause users to leave without converting.

A good landing page should be relevant to the advertisement, load quickly, communicate the offer clearly, and provide a simple conversion process.

If CTR is high but conversion rate is low, the landing page or offer should be investigated.


OFFER QUALITY

The quality and relevance of the offer can strongly influence conversions.

An attractive and relevant offer can improve conversion rate.

A weak, unclear, or poorly positioned offer can reduce conversions.

If traffic and CTR are strong but conversions remain low, marketers should review the offer.


CAMPAIGN SCALING

A campaign with consistently strong ROAS, healthy conversion rate, and acceptable CPA may be suitable for gradual budget scaling.

Budget should not be increased aggressively without monitoring performance.

If ROAS decreases significantly after increasing budget, the campaign may be reaching less efficient audience segments.


BUDGET OPTIMIZATION

Marketing budget should be allocated toward campaigns that demonstrate stronger performance.

Campaigns with low ROAS and high CPA may require optimization or budget reduction.

Campaigns with strong ROAS and healthy conversion rates may receive additional budget after performance stability is confirmed.


GENERAL DIAGNOSTIC RULES

If CTR is low and conversion rate is low:
Possible issues include poor targeting, weak creative, weak messaging, or irrelevant traffic.

If CTR is high but conversion rate is low:
Possible issues include landing page experience, offer quality, pricing, or conversion process.

If CTR is low but conversion rate is high:
The traffic that does click may be highly relevant, but the advertisement may not be attracting enough users.

If ROAS is low and CPA is high:
The campaign may be spending too much to acquire customers relative to the revenue generated.

If ROAS is high and CPA is low:
The campaign is generally showing strong acquisition efficiency.

If revenue is increasing while ROAS is stable:
The campaign may be scaling efficiently.

If revenue is increasing but ROAS is decreasing:
Revenue growth may be coming at the cost of lower advertising efficiency.

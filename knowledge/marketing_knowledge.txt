CTR measures the percentage of users who click an advertisement after seeing it.
A low CTR can indicate weak ad creatives, poor targeting, or an irrelevant message.

CPC represents the average cost paid for each click.
A high CPC can indicate expensive keywords, strong competition, or inefficient targeting.

Conversion Rate measures the percentage of clicks that result in conversions.
A low conversion rate may indicate landing page problems, weak offers, poor audience targeting, or mismatch between advertisement and landing page.

CPA represents the average cost required to acquire one customer or conversion.
A high CPA generally indicates inefficient campaign spending and should be investigated.

ROAS measures revenue generated for every unit of advertising spend.
Higher ROAS generally indicates better advertising efficiency.

When ROAS is low, marketers should review campaign targeting, creatives, landing pages, audience quality, and budget allocation.

When CTR decreases significantly, marketers should test different ad creatives, headlines, calls-to-action, and audience segments.

When conversion rate decreases while CTR remains strong, the problem may be occurring after the click, such as landing page experience, offer quality, pricing, or checkout experience.

Budget should generally be shifted toward campaigns that consistently demonstrate strong ROAS and conversion performance.

A campaign should not be evaluated using a single KPI.
CTR, CPC, conversion rate, CPA, ROAS, spend, and revenue should be considered together.

Historical performance is important when making marketing decisions.
Recent changes should be compared with previous periods to identify meaningful trends.

Marketing recommendations should consider business objectives, available budget, audience characteristics, campaign goals, and historical performance.
